The gas pedal: get ready to act

A difficult exam looks like a threat. The activates the sympathetic system to help you respond quickly.

  • Adrenal glands release (adrenaline) into the bloodstream.
  • Your heart rate, pulse, and blood pressure rise; breathing gets faster and airways open wider.
  • Alertness sharpens and (blood sugar) and stored fats become available as fuel.
In the exam room: a pounding heart and faster breathing are signs that your body is mobilizing for a challenge.

The brake: settle and restore

You hand in the exam. As the threat passes, the parasympathetic system helps the body move into its “rest and digest” state.

  • Circulating stress hormones such as fall.
  • Heart rate slows and breathing returns toward its normal rhythm.
  • Muscle tension eases as the body returns toward a balanced, relaxed baseline.
After the exam: recovery is part of a healthy stress response, not the absence of one.

03 / MAKE THE DISTINCTION

Stress is not automatically a bad thing.

The same response that helps you meet an immediate demand can become a problem when the body stays on high alert for too long.

Short-term /

A useful burst of focus and fuel.

A big test or deadline can create a short-lived response. Faster breathing and more available energy help the brain and body meet the demand. That extra push can feel motivating.

Long-term /

When “high alert” keeps going.

If activation persists, it can interfere with sleep, affect immune function, and contribute to longer-term physical and psychological problems. Ongoing anxiety can also keep the body on alert.
